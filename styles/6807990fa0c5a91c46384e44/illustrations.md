# Overview

Pyaterochka has a stable campaign illustration system built around expressive branded mascots and rounded prize or loyalty objects. It appears across Home promotion, games, coupons, and participation education. Product photography and category cutouts are separate factual imagery systems and do not become mascot illustration.

# Visual Style

Characters use simplified rounded bodies, oversized eyes, broad expressions, clean outlines, and glossy-to-soft dimensional shading. Green is the recurring world color, with red, orange, lime, yellow, sky, and purple accents. Prize objects—cards, coupons, gift boxes, headphones, game consoles, and shopping bags—share the same bright, polished, approachable treatment.

When a product requires illustration in this language, generate it with the available image-generation model and integrate the result as an image asset. Do not recreate required product illustration programmatically in SwiftUI. A screen that depends on that illustration is not ready for design approval until the generated asset is integrated in the running app.

# Composition

A hero campaign places one large mascot or character group in the upper or middle field, with the action separated below. Loyalty education pairs a short numbered step with one isolated object scene. Coupon pages may place prize objects across a saturated header while a white information sheet rises from below. Keep text-safe space and do not crop eyes, faces, or the character's main gesture.

# Color and Materials

Use saturated brand green as the dominant campaign field, with lighter radial or patterned green variation for depth. White information surfaces and type provide contrast. Red belongs to brand and commitment accents; lime can mark playful game actions. Objects use clean plastic, card, paper, or soft character materials with broad highlights and restrained shadows.

# Variants and States

Use mascots for game entry, loyalty encouragement, coupon participation, prize states, empty or gated campaign states, and promotional storytelling. Use object-only scenes for explanatory steps when a character would compete with the instruction. Keep catalog results, product detail, nutrition, cart rows, replacement settings, payment, and totals photography- or information-led.

# Avoid

- Do not replace the character family with generic emoji, stock cartoons, flat corporate people, or arbitrary SF Symbols.
- Do not turn product photography into illustration or use a mascot in place of an exact product pack shot.
- Do not place expressive characters behind prices, totals, payment choices, or replacement decisions.
- Do not shrink hero characters into small badges or crop away their face and gesture.
- Do not use muddy textures, realistic room lighting, or unrelated illustration styles inside the same campaign.
