# Overview

Simply uses a recurring authored mascot system for onboarding and identity-benefit moments. The art is a product-bearing visual layer, not a substitute for functional icons: a silver-gray anthropomorphic character stages simple financial ideas with cards, cash, transfer, or contactless props. Every new illustration must be created with an image-generation model, reviewed as a separate visual deliverable, and integrated only after approval as a raster asset.

# Visual Style

The character has an elongated, slightly animal-like head with long ears or a snouted profile, a silver-to-gray body, strong black contour work, and simplified dimensional shading. Forms are cartoon-like but deliberately rendered rather than flat clip art. Yellow accent shapes connect the art to the UI; financial props remain legible and materially distinct. Generate the complete scene as authored image artwork. Do not reconstruct it with SwiftUI shapes, `Canvas`, programmatic paths or gradients, SF Symbols, emoji, text glyphs, or assembled UI icons.

# Composition

The mascot is the focal point and normally occupies roughly one quarter to one half of the screen or content card. Use a clear central or slightly offset silhouette, generous light negative space, and a small number of oversized props. Copy sits in a clean neighboring zone rather than over detailed character features. Crops may approach an edge for energy but must preserve the face, gesture, and primary prop. Treat the artwork as foreground or supporting hero content, never as a faint decorative watermark.

# Color and Materials

Base the body on cool silver and medium gray with black outlines and restrained highlight/shadow transitions. Use saturated Simply yellow for one or two meaningful accents, with occasional blue or green only when a financial object requires it. Backgrounds remain white or near-white unless an observed dark campaign surface explicitly calls for contrast. Avoid photoreal skin, fabric, or fur; the material should feel like smooth illustrated metal or polished cartoon sculpture.

# Variants and States

Onboarding variants change pose and financial prop while retaining the same character proportions, contour weight, silver material, and yellow accent. Identity-benefit or transfer variants may introduce a card, cash, boomerang/contactless arc, or directional motion cue. Empty, error, permission, or success variants should be generated only when that product role is required; do not infer a new emotional character system from functional status icons. Before integration, export candidate raster images, obtain visual approval for the actual composition, and place the approved asset at the intended crop and scale.

# Avoid

- Do not draw the mascot with SwiftUI shapes, `Canvas`, programmatic paths, programmatic gradients, or code-generated vector assemblies.
- Do not replace authored scenes with SF Symbols, emoji, text glyphs, stock icons, or enlarged UI icon compositions.
- Do not count or imitate partner logos, navigation icons, bonus-category glyphs, bank-card renders, progress bars, badges, campaign banners, or product photography as this illustration system.
- Do not use generic corporate vector people, anime styling, realistic humans, childish sticker sheets, or dense scenic backgrounds.
- Do not integrate an image directly from generation output without a separate visual approval step.
- Do not use vector/code substitutes when the approved deliverable is a raster image asset.
