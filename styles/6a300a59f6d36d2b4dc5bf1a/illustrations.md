# Overview

Janymda uses standalone authored human illustration in login and identity-entry moments. The observed scenes show simplified hands or a seated person with a phone against a large colored circle and open white background. This system is separate from glossy service icons, product photography, banners, logos, and wallet graphics. New scenes must be created with an image-generation model, reviewed as separate visual deliverables, and integrated only after approval as raster assets.

# Visual Style

Use simplified rounded human anatomy, clean silhouette-led shapes, minimal facial detail, and soft dimensional shading. The rendering sits between polished vector illustration and gentle 3D volume: smooth edges, no heavy contour, restrained texture, and friendly contemporary proportions. Large yellow, violet, blue, or pink circular fields may frame the subject. Generate the complete scene as authored image artwork. Do not reconstruct it with SwiftUI shapes, `Canvas`, programmatic paths or gradients, SF Symbols, emoji, text glyphs, or assembled UI icons.

# Composition

The person or hands form the primary focal mass and occupy roughly one quarter to one half of the viewport, usually in the upper or middle zone. A large circle or simple color field sits behind or intersects the figure, while broad white negative space remains available for concise text and controls. Keep the phone, hand gesture, or identity cue readable at mobile scale. Avoid tiny decorative figures, crowded environments, and text placed over detailed anatomy.

# Color and Materials

Connect artwork to the UI through saturated Beeline yellow and supporting violet, blue, or pink. Skin, clothing, and devices use simplified blocks with soft shading and limited highlights. Backgrounds remain white or near-white outside the large colored field. Materials should feel smooth and friendly rather than glossy like the separate service-object system. Maintain sufficient contrast between figure, prop, and circle without black outlines.

# Variants and States

Login variants may show hands framing an identity cue or a seated figure using a phone. Preserve the same rounded anatomy, soft shading, limited palette, and large-circle composition across variants. Create permission, success, error, or empty variants only when the product specifically needs those roles; do not enlarge functional status icons into pseudo-illustrations. Export raster candidates, obtain visual approval for the actual pose, crop, palette, and scale, then integrate only the approved asset.

# Avoid

- Do not draw scenes with SwiftUI shapes, `Canvas`, programmatic paths, programmatic gradients, or code-generated vector assemblies.
- Do not replace authored figures with SF Symbols, emoji, text glyphs, stock icons, or enlarged UI icon compositions.
- Do not treat glossy 3D service icons, product photos, promotional banners, phone renders, logos, card graphics, or decorative gradients as this illustration system.
- Do not use generic corporate-vector teams, photoreal people, anime characters, heavy black outlines, clay mascots, or busy environments.
- Do not shrink the figure into decoration or crop away the hand, phone, or gesture that carries the scene.
- Do not integrate generated artwork before separate visual approval, and do not replace the approved raster asset with a programmatic substitute.
