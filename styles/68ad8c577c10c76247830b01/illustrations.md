# Overview

Sportmaster uses a recurring authored illustration language in activity, wellness, service, and selected empty states. Fire, food, water, map, and location characters or objects form a playful utility layer distinct from product photography and one-off campaign art.

When a product requires illustration in this language, generate it with the available image-generation model and integrate the result as an image asset. Do not recreate required art programmatically with SwiftUI shapes, SF Symbols, or emoji. A dependent screen is not ready for design approval until the generated asset is integrated in the running app.

# Visual Style

The art is rounded, friendly, and mascot-like, often giving a simple sport, flame, food, drink, map, or pin object a clear face or animated posture. Forms are chunky with few details, soft volume, clean edges, and toy-like depth. It can mix compact 3D-like shading with bold flat graphic areas, but remains readable at service-card scale.

# Composition

Use one focal character or a small related object group. Compact variants sit centered in rounded service or progress cards with clear negative space around labels and metrics. Larger empty/promo variants can occupy a substantial central or lower-screen area, with the headline kept separate and controls unobstructed.

Art should support, not replace, progress rings, values, or actions. Crop only peripheral props; keep the character face, primary object, and gesture complete. Product and category grids remain photography-led.

# Color and Materials

Cobalt and dark navy anchor the family. Add one or two saturated supporting hues such as orange/red for fire, cyan for water, yellow/green for food or progress, and occasional violet/coral. Surfaces are matte or softly volumetric with restrained highlights, not metallic or photorealistic.

Illustration backgrounds coordinate with white, pale-gray, cobalt, or navy UI panels. Maintain strong silhouette contrast and avoid turning every service card into a rainbow campaign block.

# Variants and States

Observed variants cover activity motivation, food/calorie and water utilities, map/address or location emptiness, and service/promo cards. Progress variants pair the character with rings or metrics; empty variants increase negative space and explanatory prominence.

Marketing stories and product promotions are a separate campaign layer and should not define new mascot variants. Extend the system only where a product state genuinely benefits from a utility character or object.

# Avoid

- Do not rebuild characters from SwiftUI circles, paths, gradients, SF Symbols, or emoji.
- Do not substitute generic corporate vectors, unrelated stock art, glossy 3D emoji, or photorealistic mascots.
- Do not place mascot art in product cards, size selection, checkout totals, or other routine commerce rows.
- Do not crowd illustrations with several unrelated sports props or ornamental copy.
- Do not shrink required art until it becomes an icon or crop away the focal face/object.
- Do not request design approval until required generated image assets appear in the running screen.
