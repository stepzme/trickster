# Overview

t2 uses a recurring authored image language across services, subscriptions, games, profile, offer, and home-product surfaces. Glossy toy-like 3D objects provide the primary visual mass, while neon outline illustrations act as a lighter category variant. Both modes share the same lime, magenta, cyan, violet, blue, black, and white identity.

# Visual Style

Primary objects are chunky, simplified, and tactile, combining glossy saturated plastic, translucent material, or chrome-like highlights. Shapes are symbolic rather than realistic and often use one strong lime, magenta, cyan, violet, or blue accent. Lighting is soft and studio-like with a restrained floor shadow. The secondary outline mode uses clean neon strokes, minimal detail, and the same symbolic subjects and palette. A compact magenta square form recurs as a branded loading motif.

Create every required illustration with an image-generation model. Generate each object or composition as an image asset, review the result, obtain explicit approval, and only then integrate it into the interface. Do not reconstruct this imagery with SwiftUI shapes, SF Symbols, ad hoc vector paths, or assembled interface icons.

# Composition

Place one object or tight object cluster centrally in a rounded tile or open hero region. The artwork typically occupies about one third to one half of a service tile and leaves a clean zone for title, status, price, or action. Use a frontal or slight three-quarter view and retain the complete silhouette. On larger dark or promotional fields, the object may become the primary visual mass but must not obscure explicit service data.

Outline variants remain centered and similarly scaled, with ample empty field around the stroke. Text and controls stay outside the generated asset. Use `contain` behavior for objects and avoid aggressive crops unless an observed promotional card clearly uses one.

# Color and Materials

Anchor the artwork in black, white, and electric lime. Assign one dominant supporting hue such as magenta, cyan, violet, or blue to a single object or service family. Use glossy plastic, translucent color, chrome highlights, or a restrained pastel platform. Maintain strong contrast and simple backgrounds; do not mix several competing gradients or material families in one tile.

# Variants and States

Observed roles include service categories, subscriptions, games, financial products, gifts or privileges, profile and More tiles, and home-connectivity products. The focal object changes by category while scale, material, studio light, palette logic, and clean label zone remain consistent. Neon outline variants serve lighter category or utility surfaces; the magenta square remains reserved for loading contexts.

Generate only the variants required by the consuming product. Every generated result requires explicit visual approval before integration. Dense balance, allowance, tariff, and transaction rows should remain illustration-free.

# Avoid

- Do not substitute stock 3D icons, emoji, generic vector people, or arbitrary SF Symbols.
- Do not reconstruct the art with SwiftUI shapes, programmatic paths, or assembled interface glyphs.
- Do not place several unrelated focal objects or multiple competing gradients in one card.
- Do not hide explicit product state, balance, price, or terms behind artwork.
- Do not crop the authored object until its symbolic silhouette becomes unclear.
- Do not embed UI copy, values, controls, or buttons inside the image asset.
- Do not integrate generated artwork before explicit approval.
