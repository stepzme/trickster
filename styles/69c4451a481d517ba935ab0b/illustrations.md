# Overview

How We Feel has a limited but coherent authored illustration family used in onboarding and selected supportive or educational surfaces. Abstract emotional characters, a book and wand, a body/cloud/lock metaphor, an outlined hand with a colored form, and dotted breathing imagery share luminous colored bodies, fine white line details, and generous black space. This family is separate from central emotion glyphs, brand marks, functional icons, charts, photography, and decorative color fields.

Create any new illustration with an image-generation model using a prompt derived from this document. The generated image must receive separate visual approval before it is added to the interface. Integrate the approved output as a raster asset. Do not recreate the art with SwiftUI shapes, SF Symbols, emoji, text glyphs, Canvas drawing, or programmatic gradients.

# Visual Style

Use digital vector-like illustration with abstract rounded bodies, smooth saturated gradients, and thin white line overlays. Character figures consist of one or two blob-like colored forms with tiny line limbs, faces, or highlights. Metaphor objects such as books, clouds, locks, hands, and wands are simplified into minimal outline geometry.

The result should feel expressive and humane without becoming cute clip-art. Surfaces may have very soft inner shading or fine noise, but no visible brushwork, paper grain, hard outline around the colored body, or photorealistic detail. Light is implied by tonal gradients rather than cast shadows.

# Composition

Place one clear emotional idea in the center or upper-middle of a black field. A hero illustration generally occupies the middle third of the screen and leaves strong negative space for a serif heading, short explanation, and lower action. On onboarding, several small figures may form a loose constellation rather than a dense scene.

Card variants use a smaller illustration as a clear accent inside a dark rounded surface. Keep the focal silhouette readable and avoid crowding nearby copy. Dotted breathing art may extend more broadly across a card, but remains quiet and secondary to the exercise title.

# Color and Materials

Use the same emotional palette defined in ui.md: luminous yellow, green, red/orange, blue/purple, and white linework on black. Yellow and green are common for supportive and educational metaphors. Gradients stay within one emotional family rather than mixing unrelated accent colors inside one body.

White lines describe faces, limbs, page marks, clouds, locks, hands, and sparkles. Black negative space is an active material, not an empty background to be filled. Avoid metallic, glass, photographic, clay-3D, or glossy app-icon materials.

# Variants and States

Onboarding may use several small emotion-character figures with simple expressions and line limbs. Educational or reflective moments use one metaphor object such as an open book with wand and sparkles or a body shape combined with cloud and lock linework. Support cards may pair a green abstract form with a white outlined hand. Breathing content may use dotted abstract fields that share the palette and restrained geometry.

No consistent authored illustration variants were observed for loading, error, permission, success, or generic empty states. Do not add illustrations to those states merely to occupy space. Recorded emotion glyphs and analytical charts follow their own functional systems and are not variants of this illustration family.

# Avoid

- Do not draw or animate the illustration directly in SwiftUI or UIKit.
- Do not substitute SwiftUI shapes, Canvas drawings, SF Symbols, emoji, text glyphs, or stacked programmatic gradients.
- Do not integrate a generated asset before separate visual approval; use only the approved raster output.
- Do not use stock photography, flat corporate line scenes, generic 3D objects, detailed cartoons, or photorealistic people.
- Do not confuse the family with emotion-selection blobs, charts, logo marks, functional icons, or user photography.
- Do not fill black negative space with decorative text, dense particles, or multiple competing metaphors.
- Do not crop away the central silhouette or shrink hero art into an incidental thumbnail.
