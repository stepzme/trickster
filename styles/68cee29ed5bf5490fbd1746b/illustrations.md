# Overview

Detsky Mir uses a recurring blue bear mascot and related family-friendly spot scenes on onboarding, authentication, notification, and empty-state surfaces. The artwork makes sparse guidance and state screens recognizable while remaining separate from product photography, campaign banners, category pictograms, and interface icons.

When a surface requires this illustration language, generate the image with an available image-generation model and integrate the resulting asset. Do not recreate it with SwiftUI shapes, SF Symbols, or emoji. The screen is not ready for design approval until the generated asset is visible in the running interface.

# Visual Style

Use friendly rounded 2D character art with the blue bear as the recurring focal subject. Shapes are broad and soft, facial expressions are simple and cheerful, and supporting props are oversized enough to read immediately at phone scale. Rendering is mostly flat with occasional gentle dimensional shading, minimal outline emphasis, and no realistic texture. Related spot art follows the same toy-like proportions and approachable emotional tone.

# Composition

State screens use one clear central or upper-middle character scene surrounded by generous white or pale-blue negative space. The artwork is a substantial mass, commonly about one quarter to one half of the available content height, with a short title, concise body, and action placed below or nearby. Wide benefit or notification modules may place the mascot and one large prop inside a rounded field while keeping an uncluttered region for text. Preserve the complete face, gesture, and meaning-carrying prop within the crop.

# Color and Materials

Brand blue and pale sky blue dominate the bear, background fields, and structural shapes. White keeps scenes open; controlled coral-red, yellow, green, and occasional purple add emphasis to a prop or state. Materials remain matte and softly graphic, with limited gentle shading rather than glossy 3D lighting. Maintain enough contrast for near-black or white adjacent text, and keep the palette related to the blue commerce shell without borrowing the loud multicolor density of product grids.

# Variants and States

Observed use spans onboarding or introductory guidance, authentication support, notification prompts, and empty states. Variants change the bear's pose, expression, and one supporting object while retaining the same proportions, blue identity, sparse setting, and friendly tone. Empty states should feel calm and explanatory; permission or notification scenes may be more active but still leave clean space for concise product text and a primary action.

# Avoid

- Do not use stock photography, photorealistic animals, glossy 3D mascots, or unrelated cartoon styles.
- Do not replace the mascot scene with an oversized SF Symbol, emoji, app icon, or category pictogram.
- Do not draw production artwork from SwiftUI shapes or assemble it from interface icons.
- Do not treat product photography or short-lived promotional banners as illustration variants.
- Do not add a busy retail grid, detailed scenery, textures, or long embedded copy behind the character.
- Do not crop away the bear's face, gesture, or the prop that communicates the state.
- Do not spread the mascot into dense catalog rows where product imagery is the required visual content.
