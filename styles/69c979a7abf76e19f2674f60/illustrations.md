# Overview

Headspace uses a stable authored illustration language as the main emotional layer across onboarding, discovery, playback, checks, results, breathing, profile statistics, empty states, and modal moments. New imagery must be generated with the available image-generation model, visually approved before integration, and inserted as the approved raster asset. Do not reproduce the language with SwiftUI shapes, `Canvas`, programmatic vectors, SF Symbols, or emoji.

# Visual Style

Use simple rounded characters and objects, soft organic silhouettes, minimal facial detail, and gentle editorial wit. Forms are flat to lightly textured rather than glossy or realistic. Linework is sparse; color blocking and recognizable posture carry the idea. Characters feel calm and human without becoming clinical diagrams.

# Composition

Build one clear visual idea per scene. Onboarding and empty states center a large figure or object with broad negative space for a short heading and action. Card art uses a readable focal subject within rounded crops. Playback expands the scene or broad color bands around a central control, keeping the control visually unobstructed. Do not crop away the gesture or primary silhouette.

# Color and Materials

Favor saturated orange and yellow, supported by pink, deep purple, blue, and limited neutral tones. Each scene uses a compact palette with one dominant field. Materials remain matte and softly textured, with subtle tonal variation rather than glass, metallic gloss, or photographic lighting. Contrast must follow the roles defined in `ui.md`.

# Variants and States

Onboarding and guidance use warm optimistic character scenes. Sleep content moves toward purple and deep-blue fields. Empty and result states reduce the scene to one centered subject with ample white space. Playback uses the broadest color treatment, while profile statistics and checks use smaller contained motifs. Across variants, retain rounded silhouettes, sparse detail, and a single visual idea.

# Avoid

- Do not use stock wellness photography, photoreal people, clinical diagrams, or glossy 3D renders.
- Do not substitute SF Symbols, emoji, SwiftUI shapes, or programmatic vectors for authored scenes.
- Do not crowd a scene with multiple competing characters or props.
- Do not place detailed art behind copy or playback controls.
- Do not mix many saturated palettes inside one card.
- Do not crop away the primary gesture, face, or silhouette.
