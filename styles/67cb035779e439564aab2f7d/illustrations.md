# Overview

Setka has a sustained illustration language for professional relationships and for a small number of empty states. The main authored motif is a network of portrait silhouettes held inside glossy violet nodes and joined by fine luminous lines on black. A secondary monochrome line-drawing style appears when search has no results. Product screenshots and user media are separate asset classes and should not be restyled as illustration.

# Visual Style

Network nodes use simplified bust silhouettes rather than detailed faces. Each silhouette sits inside a circular or slightly dimensional violet shell with a bright rim, dark center, and soft outer glow. Connections are thin straight violet lines. Small employer or service marks may attach to a node as factual relationship context, but they remain minor accents.

Empty-state art is sparse black-and-white line work with a playful editorial character. It uses a few bold filled shapes inside the outline but no colored scene, gradient card, or decorative background. Keep it visually separate from conventional interface icons.

When the design calls for product illustration, generate it with the available image-generation model and integrate the resulting image asset into the interface. Do not recreate the illustration programmatically in SwiftUI. A screen that requires illustration is not ready for design approval until the generated image asset is integrated.

# Composition

Network scenes range from a compact pair to a viewport-scale graph. One or two larger nodes establish the user's identity; smaller nodes radiate outward in an asymmetric but readable graph. Connections must remain visible without turning into a dense mesh. Black negative space around the graph protects the nearby statement and progress controls.

For a relationship sequence, grow the same visual system from a single person, to a connected pair, to a small cluster, then to a wider field. Preserve the apparent depth with overlapping node scales and glow rather than drawing a literal diagram grid.

Empty-state drawings occupy the central portion of an otherwise sparse screen. Use one scene and one short supporting statement. Do not repeat the illustration inside each result category or add unrelated decorative objects.

# Color and Materials

Black is the uninterrupted field. Nodes move through near-black, deep indigo, ultraviolet, and bright magenta-violet. Rim highlights may approach pale lavender or white. Connection lines use a restrained violet glow; they should remain thinner and dimmer than node rims.

Nodes feel like glossy illuminated glass or polished resin, not flat circles. Use controlled internal gradients, narrow highlights, and soft bloom without smoky texture or heavy cast shadows. Empty-state drawings remain near-white on black with small black fills and no violet glow.

# Variants and States

Use the glossy network language for onboarding explanations, relationship discovery, mutual-connection context, and rare identity moments. A compact card may show several small nodes; a hero explanation may expand the graph across most of the available height.

Use monochrome line art only for a true explanatory empty state. Loading, validation, permissions, routine navigation, chat actions, and settings do not require illustration. User avatars, company logos, screenshots, and post media remain supplied content rather than generated variants of the illustration system.

# Avoid

- Do not substitute generic people SF Symbols, emoji portraits, or stock business photography for network nodes.
- Do not recreate the node field with SwiftUI circles, gradients, and lines in place of an integrated generated image asset.
- Do not add detailed faces, office environments, charts, laptops, or handshake clichés.
- Do not fill every dark screen with violet glow; illustration is reserved for identity and relationship meaning.
- Do not mix the glossy violet network style with the monochrome empty-state drawing inside one composition.
- Do not copy employer marks or real portraits into generated assets unless the product supplies and authorizes them.
