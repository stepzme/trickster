# Overview

Brilliant uses a stable authored illustration system as instructional content and course identity. Rounded 3D and vector models translate abstract math, science, logic, and coding concepts into inspectable objects, while medals, league forms, course tiles, and a soft mascot-like figure extend the same language into progress and identity surfaces.

# Visual Style

Objects have compact rounded geometry, crisp silhouettes, soft shadows, and simple matte or lightly glossy materials. Depth is clear but restrained; detail is limited to what supports recognition or the learning concept. Bright blue, green, teal, yellow, and purple identify variables, courses, or progress. The system favors scales, cubes, tokens, abstract math objects, medals, and blob-like forms over realistic scenery.

Create required artwork with an image-generation model. Do not construct final illustrations from SwiftUI shapes, `Canvas`, programmatic paths or gradients, SF Symbols, emoji, text glyphs, or assembled UI icons.

# Composition

One complete model or compact object group is centered as the primary visual mass, commonly occupying a substantial middle portion of the viewport or course card. Preserve generous negative space, a clear focal relationship, and every instructional label or connection. Prompts remain above the art and answers or continuation below; text and controls are not baked into the image.

Generated candidates require separate visual approval before interface integration. After approval, integrate the approved raster asset and preserve its intended crop, scale, contrast, and focal point on compact iPhone widths and in light or dark appearance.

# Color and Materials

The art uses the UI's bright blue and green anchors with controlled yellow, purple, teal, and occasional multicolor course accents. White backgrounds emphasize clean silhouettes; dark screens retain the same objects with enough rim light or contrast to remain legible. Shadows are soft and short, surfaces are clean, and gradients support object volume or premium identity without becoming cinematic or metallic.

# Variants and States

Observed variants include educational models inside lessons, colored course identity tiles, medal or league objects, progress visuals, a mascot-like green form, and premium/paywall artwork. Lesson objects stay concept-led and complete; progress and course variants can be more emblematic. Light and dark variants preserve form and palette while adjusting surrounding contrast rather than changing style.

# Avoid

- Do not use stock photography, generic clip-art, photoreal scenes, or an unrelated 3D icon pack.
- Do not substitute SwiftUI shapes, `Canvas`, programmatic paths or gradients, SF Symbols, emoji, text glyphs, or assembled UI icons for authored artwork.
- Do not integrate an image-generation model output before explicit visual approval.
- Do not replace the approved raster asset with a programmatic approximation.
- Do not crop a scale, path, label, relation, or object needed to understand the concept.
- Do not add busy scenery, dramatic lighting, dense texture, or decorative characters that compete with the model.
- Do not bake prompts, answers, controls, or product copy into the image.
