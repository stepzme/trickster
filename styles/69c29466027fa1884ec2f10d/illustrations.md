# Overview

Twinby uses a stable family of soft-volume relationship and feature objects across onboarding, tests, the profile hub, premium surfaces, and boost screens. The family includes paired butterfly or wing forms, intertwined heart loops, a wrapped document, suitcase, lightning bolt, and flame-like action objects. These images support emotional framing and feature recognition; user photography, functional line icons, provider marks, city photos, and campaign screenshots are separate visual categories.

Every new illustration must be created with an image-generation model from a prompt derived from this document. The generated image requires separate visual approval before interface integration. After approval, integrate the exported raster asset into the interface. Do not recreate or approximate this authored art with SwiftUI shapes, SF Symbols, emoji, text glyphs, or programmatic gradients.

# Visual Style

Use a polished digital vector/3D hybrid with rounded, inflated silhouettes and smooth clay- or plastic-like volume. Forms have minimal or no outlines; gradient fill, soft inner shading, and a restrained cast shadow establish depth. Surfaces are synthetic and clean, without paper grain, brush texture, hand-drawn wobble, or photorealistic detail.

Objects are simplified enough to read as large icons or mascot-like forms. Symmetry, pairing, loops, and linked forms recur in relationship imagery. Small sparks, hearts, or fragments may accompany an object, but they remain sparse supporting details rather than a confetti field.

# Composition

Hero illustrations are large, centered focal masses in the middle or upper-middle of a full-screen purple field, leaving clear negative space for a headline and lower CTA. They float directly on the background instead of sitting inside a decorative frame. Paywall objects follow the same hierarchy at a slightly smaller scale.

Feature-tile variants occupy a large fraction of a rounded card and may bleed or crop toward the right or lower edge. Keep one dominant metaphor per image. Preserve readable silhouettes and intentional negative space; do not assemble narrative scenes or dense collections of unrelated objects.

# Color and Materials

The core palette is saturated violet, purple, lavender, and pink, coordinated with ui.md. Orange and yellow introduce energy for boosts, special likes, lightning, or flame-like objects. Green belongs mainly to functional compatibility and should appear in authored art only when the observed role requires it.

Use smooth tonal gradients, soft frontal or upper lighting, a slightly darker underside, and a diffuse cast shadow. Purple backgrounds may keep illustrations tonal rather than forcing maximum contrast. Avoid metallic realism, glass, fur, paper, grain, hard black outlines, and generic multicolor 3D icon palettes.

# Variants and States

Onboarding uses paired wing or butterfly characters with minimal closed-eye expressions and small heart details. Relationship or test moments use intertwined pink heart loops and soft shadows. Utility and profile features use isolated objects such as a purple document or suitcase. Monetization and action variants use orange lightning, flame, or similarly energetic single objects with a few spark fragments.

Loading, empty, error, permission, and success variants were not observed as part of this authored family; do not invent them solely to fill space. Use an illustration only when the product role calls for a comparable hero or feature focal point.

# Avoid

- Do not draw the illustration directly in SwiftUI or UIKit.
- Do not substitute SwiftUI shapes, SF Symbols, emoji, text glyphs, or stacked programmatic gradients.
- Do not integrate an unapproved generation; visual approval is required before the raster asset enters the interface.
- Do not use stock photography, thin corporate line art, flat clip-art, glossy generic 3D icon packs, or photorealistic scenes.
- Do not mix user portraits, city photography, provider logos, or campaign screenshots into this authored family.
- Do not add dense backgrounds, multiple competing metaphors, excessive particles, or detailed environments.
- Do not crop away the primary silhouette or reduce hero art to a small decorative thumbnail.
