# Overview

Tinkoff Journal uses a stable authored illustration language on learning and course surfaces. It explains ideas, distinguishes lessons, and creates the dominant mass of course cards; it is not decoration for every screen.

# Visual Style

Flat two-dimensional cartoon drawing uses loose dark outlines, simplified people and everyday objects, playful proportions, and economical detail. Shapes feel hand-drawn rather than geometrically perfect. Pose and silhouette carry expression. Educational metaphors and lightly humorous object combinations recur.

# Composition

On course cards, art occupies the broad upper region or at least one third of the card, with one focal group and open space for a title. Lesson and quiz art sits centered above or beside the prompt and remains authored content, not an icon. Bold edge crops are allowed if the explanatory subject remains legible.

# Color and Materials

Use sky blue, mint, green, warm yellow, lavender, coral, and off-white with near-black outlines. Color is matte and mostly solid, with minimal shading and no glossy 3D material. The palette supports white editorial UI and is softer than interactive cobalt.

# Variants and States

Observed variants cover course promotion, lesson explanation, quiz prompts, and end-of-lesson feedback. All share the outline, simplification, and pastel palette while changing the central metaphor. Progress or rating states may reduce art scale to leave room for controls, but do not replace it with a symbol.

# Avoid

Avoid stock photography, photorealistic renders, glossy 3D mascots, dense comic scenes, perfect vector geometry, emoji, SF Symbols, or generic blobs standing in for art. When a surface requires illustration, generate it with the available image-generation model and integrate the image asset. Do not recreate it with SwiftUI shapes, SF Symbols, or emoji. The screen is not ready for design approval until the generated asset is visible in the running interface.
