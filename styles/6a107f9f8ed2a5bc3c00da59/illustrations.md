# Overview

Kulikov uses a repeatable family of glossy purple 3D objects for loyalty, tips, challenges, gifts, and promotional engagement. These authored objects support product photography but do not replace it in the catalog.

# Visual Style

Use an image-generation model to create original rounded 3D objects with inflated proportions, smooth high-gloss surfaces, soft studio lighting, and controlled reflections. Motifs may include a game controller, gift, coupon, shopping bag, challenge token, or phone-like object, simplified into friendly toy-scale forms. Generate the image asset, obtain visual approval, and only then integrate the approved raster asset. Do not draw the art with SwiftUI `Shape`, `Canvas`, programmatic vectors, SF Symbols, emoji, or other programmatic substitutes.

# Composition

Use one dominant object or a compact two-object cluster. The art usually occupies roughly one third to one half of a promotional card, aligned opposite a short text block or centered as the main visual mass. Preserve generous negative space, a readable silhouette, and intentional edge crop; do not scatter many small objects. Art is supporting content for loyalty and engagement, while product photography remains primary in commerce cards.

# Color and Materials

Anchor objects in saturated violet and magenta with lighter lavender highlights, occasional warm pink accents, and soft neutral reflections. Materials are glossy polymer or glass-like plastic rather than matte clay. Lighting is broad and soft, with one clear highlight direction and a subtle grounding shadow. Backgrounds stay pale lilac, blush, cream, or transparent for placement on the surfaces defined in `ui.md`.

# Variants and States

Use closely related object motifs for tips, challenges, rewards, gifts, and promotional cards. Success or earned variants may become brighter and add a small celebratory companion object; informational variants remain calmer and single-object. Product, cart, and checkout states continue to use real dessert photography rather than converting products into illustrations.

# Avoid

- Do not use SwiftUI shapes, `Canvas`, programmatic vector art, SF Symbols, emoji, or icon-font substitutes.
- Do not integrate an unapproved generated image; generation must be followed by visual approval and approved raster-asset integration.
- Avoid flat vector cartoons, stock 3D icon packs, hard metallic surfaces, photorealistic human characters, noisy scenes, and inconsistent light directions.
- Do not shrink the authored object into a decorative corner icon or place it behind essential text.
- Do not replace dessert photography with 3D illustrations.
