# Overview

Stars Coffee uses a repeatable authored illustration system for onboarding and selected brand/loyalty moments: flowing teal fields, oversized cream stars, cups, hands, phones, and friendly soft-dimensional objects. Catalog products and news remain photography/campaign content.

# Visual Style

Use an image-generation model to create original simplified dimensional scenes with smooth teal waves, cream starbursts, rounded hands/devices/cups, soft 3D-like volume, and warm playful proportions. Lighting is broad and gentle, with clean silhouettes and few objects. Generate the image, obtain visual approval, and integrate only the approved raster asset. Do not use SwiftUI `Shape`, `Canvas`, programmatic vectors/gradients, SF Symbols, emoji, or other programmatic substitutes.

# Composition

Build each scene around one hero object or a compact object pair, typically occupying one third to one half of the viewport. Use one broad curved wave as a background field and a few floating stars as accents. Preserve a large quiet zone for the display headline and action. Do not scatter many small icons or hide the hero behind UI controls.

# Color and Materials

Use dark forest teal, bright turquoise, cream, and warm coffee brown. Objects feel like soft polished plastic or paper-clay with restrained highlights, not hard metallic 3D. Stars and text zones stay cream/white for contrast; avoid unrelated saturated hues. Shadows are soft and shallow.

# Variants and States

Onboarding uses the largest hero objects and sweeping waves. Loyalty education or milestones may use a cup, star, hand, or device in a smaller card composition. Empty/celebratory moments can add a few stars while preserving the same material and palette. Menu products, flowers, store imagery, and gift-card photography remain authentic content assets.

# Avoid

- Do not use SwiftUI shapes, `Canvas`, programmatic vectors or gradients, SF Symbols, emoji, or icon-font substitutes.
- Do not integrate generated art before visual approval; follow image-generation → approval → approved raster integration.
- Avoid flat stock vector packs, photorealistic people, hard metallic objects, detailed scenes, excessive stars, and colors outside teal/cream/brown.
- Do not use authored art as a replacement for product photography.
- Do not place key text over the hero object or brightest wave.
