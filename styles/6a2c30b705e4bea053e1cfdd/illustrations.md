# Overview

MegaPay uses a stable object-based illustration system: isolated glossy 3D objects provide recognizable anchors for service categories, product states, promotions, and empty content. This is not a character narrative system. New artwork must be produced with an image-generation model, reviewed as a separate visual deliverable, and integrated only after approval as a raster asset.

# Visual Style

Objects resemble polished plastic, coated metal, or soft molded material, with rounded geometry, clean bevels, controlled reflections, and soft studio shadows. Typical motifs include floating cards, a folder or document, gift bag, car, camera, wallet props, and directional investment forms. The rendering is simplified and brand-led rather than photoreal. Generate each complete object or scene as image artwork. Do not reconstruct it with SwiftUI shapes, `Canvas`, programmatic paths or gradients, SF Symbols, emoji, text glyphs, or assembled UI icons.

# Composition

Use one primary object or a very small coherent cluster, centered or slightly offset within a white or pale tile. Favor front-three-quarter or mild isometric angles and enough negative space for a concise label. The object is usually the primary visual mass of its module, with a soft shadow grounding it and no busy environment. Empty-state objects may float in a larger open field; service objects remain compact but clearly legible. Preserve full silhouettes unless the source composition deliberately crops a large object.

# Color and Materials

Anchor the palette in MegaPay green and mint, supported by white, pale gray, dark navy, and occasional purple or blue. Use smooth gradient lighting within the raster render, subtle translucency where materially plausible, and diffuse shadows rather than hard outlines. Backgrounds should be transparent, white, or a very pale UI-compatible tint. Keep saturation concentrated in the object so the surrounding interface remains quiet.

# Variants and States

Category variants change the central object while maintaining the same camera angle family, material gloss, lighting softness, shadow scale, and negative space. Empty states may use a floating-card or folder composition at a larger scale. Promotional variants can add a second supporting object, but should not become collage banners. Success, error, permission, or progress objects should be created only when the product needs that role; do not enlarge functional status glyphs into pseudo-illustrations. Export candidate raster images, obtain visual approval for the actual object, crop, and scale, then integrate only the approved asset.

# Avoid

- Do not build objects with SwiftUI shapes, `Canvas`, programmatic paths, programmatic gradients, or code-generated 3D/vector assemblies.
- Do not replace authored objects with SF Symbols, emoji, text glyphs, stock icons, or enlarged functional UI icons.
- Do not treat the MegaPay mark, partner logos, category glyphs, QR/camera UI, donut charts, human photography, campaign banners, or background gradients as this illustration system.
- Do not use generic clay-character packs, photoreal stock products, flat corporate vectors, hard black outlines, or crowded scenic backgrounds.
- Do not mix unrelated camera angles, lighting directions, or material finishes across a set.
- Do not integrate generated artwork before separate visual approval, and do not replace the approved raster asset with a programmatic substitute.
