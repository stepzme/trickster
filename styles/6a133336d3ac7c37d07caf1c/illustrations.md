# Overview

Yandex Music uses a stable authored family of abstract neon waves, translucent 3D/glass-like category objects, blurred spectral fields, and star/sun motifs. These graphics create branded onboarding, discovery, category, and education moments alongside authentic music artwork.

# Visual Style

Use an image-generation model to create original abstract forms with rounded volume, translucent or glossy material, internal glow, soft refraction, and blurred spectral light. Motifs may suggest waves, ribbons, stars, sound, motion, or category ideas without becoming literal interface icons. Generate each image, obtain visual approval, and integrate only the approved raster asset. Do not reproduce these graphics with SwiftUI `Shape`, `Canvas`, programmatic gradients/vectors, SF Symbols, emoji, or other programmatic substitutes.

# Composition

Use one oversized object or a tightly controlled cluster as a primary visual mass. On onboarding or education screens, the art may occupy roughly one third to one half of the viewport and sit beneath or opposite a large title. On category cards it is cropped boldly at an edge while leaving clear space for a short label. On dark listening surfaces, the object may float centrally in broad negative space. Do not use the art as a tiny corner decoration or place essential text over its brightest focal area.

# Color and Materials

Use saturated cyan, electric green, violet, magenta, orange, and yellow in smooth spectral transitions. Materials feel like illuminated glass, gel, or polished translucent plastic with soft bloom and controlled highlights. Black backgrounds increase neon contrast; white backgrounds use softer shadows and sufficient edge separation. The yellow star motif remains a focused brand accent rather than flooding every object.

# Variants and States

Onboarding and modal education use large singular objects with generous negative space. Discovery/category variants use more compact cropped glyphs and varied spectral palettes. Branded transitions may use blurred wave fields or star motifs. Music content itself continues to use authentic album/playlist artwork, and playback states should sample content color rather than replacing covers with abstract illustration.

# Avoid

- Do not use SwiftUI shapes, `Canvas`, programmatic vectors or gradients, SF Symbols, emoji, or icon-font substitutes for authored art.
- Do not integrate a generated image before visual approval; use the image-generation → approval → approved raster integration sequence.
- Avoid flat stock vectors, cartoon characters, generic music-note icons, hard mechanical geometry, noisy particle fields, and inconsistent material lighting.
- Do not turn album covers into illustrations or crop away their identifying content.
- Do not scatter many small decorative objects or place bright forms behind essential labels and controls.
