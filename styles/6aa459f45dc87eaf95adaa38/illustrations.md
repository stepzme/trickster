# Overview

Fresh Screen Gallery research independently confirms a stable authored Wabi image system: glossy translucent spheres, orb-contained photos, rendered mini objects, and toy-like 3D thumbnails recur in onboarding, create grids, profile grids, and feed mini-app previews. These assets are part of the product's recognisable surface language, not optional decoration.

Production contract: final artwork must be produced with an image-generation model, reviewed as raster imagery, explicitly approved by the user visually, and only then integrated as raster assets into the app.

# Visual Style

The style is soft 3D glass with inflated circular forms, frosted or clear shells, refraction, specular highlights, and contact shadows. Contents can be photographic avatars, miniature tools, terrain, abstract iridescent blobs, or rendered game-like scenes. The forms are polished and tactile rather than hand-drawn, mascot-led, or vector-flat.

# Composition

Artwork usually appears as a loose orbital cluster above centered onboarding text, a single circular thumbnail inside a white card, or a large rounded interactive canvas on the black feed. Individual objects need generous negative space so the glossy edge remains readable. Crops should preserve circular silhouettes for thumbnails and tall rounded-rectangle clipping for feed canvases.

# Color and Materials

The imagery supplies most of the color against Wabi's white and black UI. Use cyan glass, violet, warm yellow, orange, teal, smoke, and iridescent highlights as material reflections or embedded object color. Avoid solid decorative backgrounds behind every object; keep the material contrast between pearl canvas, black stage, and glossy raster art.

# Variants and States

Use the same authored raster language for onboarding clusters, mini-app icons, draft thumbnails, profile object cards, credit/empty moments, and feed canvases. State changes may add small badges, count chips, dim overlays, or frosted tooltips, but should not swap to a different illustration idiom.

# Avoid

- Do not substitute SwiftUI shapes for authored artwork.
- Do not substitute SF Symbols for mini-app art, onboarding bubbles, object thumbnails, or feed canvases.
- Do not use emoji as artwork replacements.
- Do not use procedural gradients, flat vector blobs, mascot packs, or stock illustrations in place of the glassy raster objects.
- Do not integrate generated artwork before explicit visual approval from the user.
