# Overview

Wink uses a recurring black-and-white empty-state character with a television-like head and question-mark motif across otherwise sparse dark screens. It appears in multiple library and reminder states while film posters, games, books, and promotional campaigns retain separate content-specific artwork.

# Visual Style

The character is monochrome line-and-fill art with a compact, slightly playful silhouette: a dark or outlined body, a boxy screen-like head, simple facial or question-mark detail, and minimal shading. The drawing reads clearly against the black application field through white fills and outlines. Keep the rendering graphic and sparse rather than dimensional, textured, or photorealistic.

# Composition

Center one character treatment in the open upper-middle or middle of an empty dark screen, typically occupying roughly a quarter of the usable height. Place a short title and supporting text below it, followed by at most one primary action. Preserve broad black negative space and do not crop the television head, question mark, or outer silhouette. The art supports the empty state; it does not become a full-screen background.

# Color and Materials

Use black, white, and restrained gray as the core palette so the character belongs to the dark shell. A small orange or coral detail may echo the primary action only when observed, but the illustration should not become a multicolor campaign asset. Avoid gradients, glossy 3D material, photographic texture, colored ambient lighting, or detailed environments.

# Variants and States

Observed variants reuse the same character language for empty continue-watching, downloads, watch-now, reminders, and subscription-related states. Variation comes from pose, held object, screen/question-mark detail, or adjacent small prop while scale, monochrome treatment, central placement, and sparse composition remain consistent. Certificates, games, audiobooks, recommendations, and promotional modules use separate art direction and should not be forced into this character system.

# Avoid

- Generate new character artwork with an image-generation model and obtain approval before integration; do not rebuild it from SwiftUI shapes, SF Symbols, or ad hoc vector primitives.
- Do not replace the TV-headed figure with a generic empty-box icon, streaming logo, emoji, stock character, or colorful mascot.
- Do not add complex scenes, multiple characters, dense props, texture, or photorealistic lighting.
- Do not crop the head, question-mark motif, or silhouette, and do not enlarge the art until it overwhelms the empty-state copy.
- Do not apply this monochrome character language to posters, content promos, certificates, games, books, or campaign artwork.
