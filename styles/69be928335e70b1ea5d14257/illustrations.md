# Overview

InDrive has a stable authored illustration system built from expressive black line drawings and a single irregular acid-lime geometric mass. It appears in onboarding, geolocation education, passenger or role selection, intercity guidance, safety tiles, and driver safety explanations. Small vehicle photographs or service thumbnails are separate functional imagery and are not part of this illustration grammar.

# Visual Style

People and objects are simplified into confident black contours with sparse interior detail. Figures remain mostly white or neutral, while one large lime block supplies the brand color and visual energy. Poses are clear and slightly expressive, but the result stays editorial and instructional rather than cartoon-dense. There is little texture, no elaborate environment, and no need for photorealistic lighting.

Final artwork must be produced with an image-generation model from an explicit scene and style brief. Candidate images must be reviewed and approved before being integrated into the interface. Do not recreate the illustration with SwiftUI shapes, SF Symbols, programmatic paths, emoji, or improvised icon assemblies.

# Composition

Use one person, compact pair, or single explanatory object as the focal subject. Place it centrally or slightly offset over one angular or organic lime shape, leaving broad white or pale-gray negative space for a short title, explanation, and action. The lime block should read as one coherent visual mass rather than many decorative fragments.

On full explanatory screens the art can occupy roughly a quarter to a third of the visible content area. In safety tiles or modal panels, use a compact crop that preserves the figure's gesture and the lime silhouette. Art supports a specific explanation; it should not become wallpaper behind dense controls or live map content.

# Color and Materials

Black, white, pale gray, and acid lime are the core palette. Linework is near-black and crisp; figures and objects remain mostly unfilled or lightly neutral. The lime backdrop matches the primary action family and carries the strongest color. Occasional restrained secondary color may appear only when present in an approved generated asset, but it must not compete with lime. Avoid glossy 3D materials, photographic texture, and atmospheric gradients in this system.

# Variants and States

- Onboarding and permission education: one clear figure or object with a large lime backdrop and ample white space.
- Passenger, role, or service guidance: a compact human scene whose gesture explains the choice without additional decorative props.
- Intercity and instructional screens: a slightly wider scene, still limited to one coherent lime mass and sparse black linework.
- Safety tiles and modal explanations: tighter crops with the same line weight, neutral figure treatment, and lime support shape.

No independent illustrated success, error, or progress grammar was confirmed. Those states should use the same family only when an approved scene exists rather than inventing a new visual style.

# Avoid

- Do not draw the art with SwiftUI shapes, SF Symbols, programmatic paths, emoji, or assembled stock icons.
- Do not integrate generated candidates before the artwork itself is reviewed and approved.
- Do not substitute glossy 3D scenes, stock photography, generic corporate vectors, or multicolored cartoon packs for the line-and-lime system.
- Do not fragment the lime backdrop into many small decorations or gradients.
- Do not fill the scene with environmental detail, props, labels, or decorative copy.
- Do not crop away the gesture, face, or relationship between the subject and lime shape.
- Do not place authored art over live maps, dense forms, driver rows, or status data where it would reduce clarity.
