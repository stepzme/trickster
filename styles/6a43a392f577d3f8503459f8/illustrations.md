# Overview

Yandex Pay uses imagery to give financial products and promotions a tangible identity. The recurring language is polished, toy-like 3D object art supported by occasional product photography and merchant assets. Illustration is not continuous decoration: it appears at deliberate moments where the interface introduces a product, explains a benefit, differentiates a category, or creates a campaign/game environment.

# Visual Style

Objects are simplified and chunky, with rounded silhouettes, clear frontal or three-quarter views, and little fine detail. Materials alternate between soft matte plastic, glossy inflatable forms, translucent glass, metallic accents, and fabric-like product renders. Lighting is diffuse and studio-like, producing soft internal highlights rather than hard cast shadows.

The art often combines one recognisable object with an abstract brand token: coins and a case for credit, a translucent safe or wallet for savings, gift and Plus marks for rewards, or oversized merchant/product imagery for shopping. Small utility art remains readable at icon scale; campaign and onboarding scenes can layer several objects into a more theatrical composition.

# Composition

There are four recurring scales:

- **Hero:** art occupies roughly the middle third to half of onboarding or campaign screens and may overlap a gradient field while leaving separate space for headline and action.
- **Banner:** one object cluster sits at the trailing edge and occupies about a quarter to a third of the module; text stays in a protected area.
- **Tile/icon:** a single centred object or mark is isolated on transparent space or a very pale field and remains legible beside compact labels.
- **Environment:** games and special promotions use illustration as the full-screen world, with controls and result panels layered above it.

Cropping is purposeful: large objects may exit a card edge, but the recognisable silhouette and branded token remain visible. Dense text must not be printed over a busy object cluster. Photography in store cards is editorial and fills the image area; it should not be blended into the toy-object language indiscriminately.

# Color and Materials

Illustration takes its base from the UI gradients: pale violet, sky blue, pink, warm yellow, mint, and lime. Saturated pink-purple Plus marks and green Split marks provide small focal accents. Neutral objects use pearl white, graphite, or silver so they remain distinct on both the white canvas and colored fields.

Gradients should feel luminous and atmospheric, with broad transitions rather than narrow rainbow bands. Translucent objects need visible edge highlights and internal refraction; plastic objects need soft specular highlights; metallic details stay secondary. Dark mode preserves the object colors and introduces enough rim light or pale backing surface to prevent silhouettes from disappearing.

# Variants and States

Onboarding and product introductions use the largest and most layered compositions. Dashboard recommendations reduce the same language to one compact trailing object. Savings uses calmer translucent objects and cool ambient fields. Rewards become brighter and more graphic, mixing campaign lettering, tokens, gifts, and merchant marks. The game expands the palette into a saturated environment with colourful stacked pieces, glow, and a separate reward card.

Success is communicated with a small check, positive accent, or resolved product object rather than confetti on every completion. Error and unavailable states remain restrained and may use a warning mark inside the affected product module. Biometric and other system states use native system imagery instead of imitating the product illustration style.

# Avoid

- Do not replace product objects with a generic monochrome SF Symbols set.
- Do not use flat corporate-vector characters, hand-drawn doodles, or stock fintech scenes with people and charts.
- Do not scatter decorative objects through transaction lists, profile rows, or other reading-heavy screens.
- Do not shrink hero art into a small badge; preserve its intended color mass and overlap.
- Do not apply heavy black shadows, photorealistic room lighting, gritty textures, or sharp-edged low-poly geometry.
- Do not treat merchant photography and branded 3D objects as one interchangeable asset category.
