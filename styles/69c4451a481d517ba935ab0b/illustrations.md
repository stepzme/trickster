# Overview

The illustration system turns emotions into friendly abstract characters and tactile geometric symbols. It is playful and humane, but stays visually integrated with the black interface and the four-color emotional model.

# Visual Style

Use simple rounded forms with subtle gradients, soft dimensional shading, and sparse white line details. Characters are built from one or two asymmetric geometric bodies with tiny faces, arms, and legs. Supporting drawings use thin white outlines and minimal internal detail. Keep the result expressive rather than cute or ornamental.

# Composition

Place one clear emotional idea in the center of generous black space. On onboarding screens, combine two to four characters into a small narrative scene. On tool cards, use a single line drawing or quiet geometric pattern behind the title. For mood symbols, let the silhouette itself carry meaning and remain recognizable at small sizes.

# Color and Materials

Build illustrations from the same yellow, green, blue, and red families used by the mood system. Gradients may shift within one family, but should not introduce unrelated accent colors. Use white for line work and black for negative space; muted variants belong on inactive or background elements.

# Variants and States

Use character scenes for onboarding and supportive explanations, individual geometric symbols for recorded emotions, and restrained line drawings for exercise categories. Illustration should clarify emotional tone or make an abstract practice tangible; it should not decorate settings, forms, or dense data screens.

# Production Requirements

Use only the formats and crops supported by the implemented placement. Where the documented imagery is compositionally important, temporary placeholders must preserve its placement, crop, scale, negative space, and approximate visual weight until final art exists. No unobserved light/dark variants or export ratios are prescribed.

# Avoid

- Do not replace the documented image language with unrelated stock art, arbitrary symbols, or a denser composition.
